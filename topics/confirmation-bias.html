Confirmation Bias

The tendency to search for, interpret, and recall information that confirms preexisting beliefs

Confirmation bias is the mind's immune system: it protects your beliefs from threatening information.

We don't see things as they are; we see things as we are - and then we look for evidence that we're right.

The first principle is that you must not fool yourself - and you are the easiest person to fool.

Confirmation bias turns the internet into a personalized echo chamber where everyone agrees with you.

When you want something to be true, your brain becomes a lawyer building a case, not a scientist testing a hypothesis.

Confirmation bias explains why two intelligent people can look at the same evidence and reach opposite conclusions.

The most dangerous effect of confirmation bias is that it feels like objective reasoning while it's happening.

We remember the hits and forget the misses, creating a narrative of accuracy that doesn't exist.

Confirmation bias is why facts don't change minds. The mind changes facts to fit the mind.

The antidote to confirmation bias is actively seeking out people who disagree with you and listening without preparing your rebuttal.

Confirmation bias is the cognitive tax we pay for having beliefs at all.

Your brain is not a truth-seeking machine; it's a belief-confirming machine.

Confirmation bias makes us overvalue evidence that supports our views and undervalue evidence that challenges them.

The test of a first-rate intelligence is the ability to hold two opposed ideas in mind and still retain the ability to function.

Confirmation bias is strongest when the belief is tied to identity. Challenge the belief, and you challenge the person.

We are all conspiracy theorists about something. Confirmation bias is the thread that connects us all.

Confirmation bias is not a flaw in thinking; it's a feature of how thinking works. The flaw is not recognizing it.

The most honest people are those who actively work to disprove their own beliefs.

Confirmation bias explains why we stay in bad relationships, bad jobs, and bad investments - we look for signs that it's working.

The scientific method is humanity's best defense against confirmation bias because it demands disconfirmation.

Confirmation bias is the reason "I knew it all along" feels so satisfying. The mind retrofits certainty.

To overcome confirmation bias, ask "What would prove me wrong?" instead of "What proves me right?"

Confirmation bias is the silent partner in every bad decision you've ever made.

The opposite of confirmation bias is not neutrality but curiosity about one's own errors.

Confirmation bias is the reason we find patterns in randomness and meaning in coincidence.
