Negativity Bias

The brain's tendency to give more weight to negative experiences than positive ones

Negativity bias is the reason one criticism can ruin a day full of compliments.

The brain is like Velcro for negative experiences and Teflon for positive ones.

Negativity bias served our ancestors well: remembering the one poisonous berry was more important than remembering the hundred safe ones.

Negativity bias is why bad news sells and good news is ignored.

We remember insults for years and compliments for minutes. That's negativity bias at work.

Negativity bias is the evolutionary scar of living in a dangerous world. It kept us alive but now it keeps us anxious.

The mind is a survival machine, not a happiness machine. Negativity bias is proof of that.

Negativity bias explains why we obsess over what went wrong and take for granted what went right.

Negativity bias is the reason one negative interaction can define a relationship.

To overcome negativity bias, deliberately savor positive experiences. Make them stick.

Negativity bias is why we expect the worst even when the evidence suggests otherwise.

Gratitude practice is the antidote to negativity bias. It trains the brain to notice what's going right.

Negativity bias is the reason social media algorithms amplify outrage. It gets our attention.

The brain processes negative information more thoroughly than positive information. It's not your fault; it's your wiring.

Negativity bias is why we replay embarrassing moments from years ago while forgetting our triumphs.

Negativity bias is the default setting of the mind. Happiness requires deliberately adjusting the settings.

Negativity bias makes us overestimate threats and underestimate opportunities.

Negativity bias is why a single negative review can outweigh dozens of positive ones in our decision-making.

Negativity bias is not a character flaw; it's a feature of human cognition. But it can be managed.

Negativity bias is the reason "what if it goes wrong" feels more real than "what if it goes right."

Negativity bias is the cognitive tax we pay for having survived as a species.

Negativity bias is why criticism stings more than praise soothes. The ratio is about five to one.

Negativity bias makes us remember the one person who disliked our work more than the hundred who loved it.

Negativity bias is the invisible hand that tilts our perception toward the dark.

Awareness of negativity bias is the first step toward correcting for it.
