Illusory Superiority

The tendency to overestimate one's own qualities and abilities relative to others

Illusory superiority is why most people think they're above average drivers, above average lovers, and above average at their jobs.

Everyone thinks they're the exception to the rule. That's the rule.

Illusory superiority is the cognitive bias that makes us believe we're better than we actually are.

The average person believes they are above average. This is mathematically impossible but psychologically inevitable.

Illusory superiority explains why everyone thinks they have a great sense of humor but few think others do.

We judge ourselves by our intentions and others by their actions. No wonder we come out ahead.

Illusory superiority is the enemy of growth. You can't improve what you think is already superior.

The Dunning-Kruger effect is illusory superiority applied to competence: the less you know, the better you think you are.

Illusory superiority is why we're shocked when we fail - we genuinely believed we were better prepared.

Humility is the antidote to illusory superiority, but humility is hard to cultivate when you think you're already humble.

Illusory superiority is the cognitive foundation of narcissism.

We all live in the center of our own universe. Illusory superiority is just the gravitational pull of that perspective.

Illusory superiority makes feedback feel like an attack rather than a gift.

The gap between how we see ourselves and how others see us is the territory of illusory superiority.

Illusory superiority is why everyone thinks they could run the country better than the people actually doing it.

We are all biased toward ourselves. Recognizing this is the first step toward seeing clearly.

Illusory superiority is the comfortable delusion that keeps us from confronting our mediocrity.

The truly superior don't need to feel superior. Illusory superiority is the crutch of the insecure.

Illusory superiority is why we're so bad at self-assessment. We grade ourselves on a curve that always favors us.

Calibration requires feedback. Without it, illusory superiority runs unchecked.

Illusory superiority is the reason "common sense" is neither common nor sensible.

We overestimate our contribution to group projects and underestimate others'. That's illusory superiority in action.

Illusory superiority is the invisible lens that makes our own flaws seem small and others' flaws seem large.

True confidence is quiet. Illusory superiority is loud.

The first step to wisdom is admitting you might not be as smart, kind, or capable as you think.
