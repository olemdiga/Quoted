Torvalds

Pragmatic sovereignty in code: the architect of Linux on craftsmanship, integrity, and the will to build

Talk is cheap. Show me the code.

I'm generally a very pragmatic person: that which works, works.

Don't ever make the mistake of thinking that you can design something better than what you get from a massive parallel evolutionary process.

Software is like sex: it's better when it's free.

Most good programmers do programming not because they expect to get paid or get adulation by the public, but because it is fun to program.

I will, in fact, claim that the difference between a bad programmer and a good one is whether they consider their code or their data structures more important. Bad programmers worry about the code. Good programmers worry about data structures and their relationships.

An infinite number of monkeys typing into GNU emacs would never make a good program.

Given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow.

The Linux philosophy is 'Laugh in the face of danger'. Oops. Wrong one. 'Do it yourself'. Yes, that's the one.

Intelligence is the ability to avoid doing work, yet getting the work done.

I like offending people, because I think people who get offended should be offended.

If you need more than three levels of indentation, you're screwed anyway, and should fix your program.

Nobody should start to undertake a large project. You start with a small trivial project, and you should never expect it to get large. If you do, you'll just overdesign and generally think it is more important than it is. Or it will not get large. That's even worse.

I don't try to be a nice person. That doesn't work. I try to be a fair person.

The thing with Linux is that it's not about me. It's about the technology and the community.

Perfection is reached not when there is nothing more to add, but when there is nothing left to take away.

That's what makes Linux so good: you put in something, and that effort multiplies. It's a snowball effect.

I've never regretted not being a jerk. I've regretted being a jerk. So I try not to be one.

The most important part of an operating system is not the kernel. It's the users.

In real open source, you have the right to control your own destiny.

I do not think that proprietary software is immoral. I just think that open source works better.

If you think your users are idiots, only idiots will use it.

There is no such thing as a perfect design. The only thing you can do is to make it good enough and then iterate.

A person who writes code that only he understands is not a genius. He is a liability.
