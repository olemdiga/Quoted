Autism

A different operating system, not a broken one

I am not broken. I am running a different operating system on hardware that was never designed for it.

The problem is not the sensory input. The problem is the lack of a filter that others seem to have been born with.

Social exhaustion is not shyness. It is the CPU running at maximum capacity to emulate a chipset it was never meant to run.

Stimming is not a flaw. It is a system defragmentation routine running in the background.

The autistic mind does not lack empathy. It lacks the bandwidth to process and display it in real time according to neurotypical protocol.

Masking is the art of running a virtual machine of a different person while your native OS idles in the background, consuming resources.

Special interests are not obsessions. They are the native language of a mind designed for depth over breadth.

The world is too loud, too bright, too fast. This is not a complaint. It is a hardware specification.

I do not process social information in real time. I batch process it over 48 to 72 hours. The delay is not avoidance. It is compilation.

Routine is not rigidity. It is a pre-compiled script that conserves executive function for tasks that actually matter.

The autistic mind is a depth-first search algorithm in a world optimized for breadth-first social crawling.

Small talk is a protocol I never received the handshake for. I am not rude. I am waiting for the payload.

My silence is not emptiness. It is a process running that you cannot see.

The burnout is real. It is not laziness. It is the system overheating after running the emulator too long.

I notice patterns others dismiss as noise. This is not paranoia. It is a higher sampling rate.

Eye contact is a DDoS attack on my sensory processing. I can listen, or I can look. Choose one.

The autistic mind is not a deficit. It is a specialization. You do not ask a supercomputer to also be good at casual conversation.

I am not anti-social. I am pro-quiet. There is a difference.

The loneliness of autism is not the absence of people. It is the presence of people who cannot read your source code.

Acceptance is not about curing the operating system. It is about building an environment that supports its native functions.

The goal is not to pass for normal. The goal is to conserve enough bandwidth to do the work only this configuration can do.

Autism is not a tragedy. It is a different way of parsing reality. The tragedy is a world that only accepts one file format.
