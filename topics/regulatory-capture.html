Regulatory Capture

When the watchdog becomes the lapdog of the regulated

Regulatory capture is what happens when the fox is put in charge of the henhouse security system.

Any regulatory agency will eventually come to serve the interests of the industry it was created to regulate.

Regulatory capture is not corruption in the traditional sense; it's the gradual alignment of worldviews between regulator and regulated.

The revolving door between regulatory agencies and the industries they oversee is the engine of regulatory capture.

Regulations designed to protect consumers often become barriers to entry that protect incumbents.

Complex regulations favor those with the resources to navigate them - which is never the public.

Regulatory capture turns public servants into industry servants without anyone noticing the transition.

The most effective form of regulatory capture is when the industry writes the regulations itself.

Regulatory agencies begin as watchdogs and end as lapdogs - the transformation is gradual and invisible.

The public interest is the first casualty of regulatory capture.

Regulatory capture explains why fines for corporate misconduct are often just a cost of doing business.

Complex systems of regulation create jobs for regulators and compliance officers but rarely improve outcomes.

The most dangerous regulatory capture is invisible: when regulators genuinely believe they're serving the public while serving industry.

Regulatory capture is the reason incumbent industries often support new regulations that crush smaller competitors.

Information asymmetry is the weapon of regulatory capture: the industry always knows more than the regulator.

Regulatory capture turns public power into private profit without anyone casting a vote.

The FDA, SEC, FCC - every regulatory agency eventually develops Stockholm syndrome toward its regulated industry.

Regulatory capture is not a bug in the system; it's a predictable feature of any regulatory framework.

Sunlight is the best disinfectant against regulatory capture, but regulated industries prefer to operate in shadow.

The only defense against regulatory capture is constant vigilance and regular rotation of oversight.

Regulatory capture is the silent killer of free markets and consumer protection.

When regulators and regulated share the same social circles, capture is inevitable.

Regulatory capture is the process by which public interest is slowly replaced by private interest.

The appearance of regulation is often more important to politicians than actual regulation - and capture ensures this.

Regulatory capture turns the state's coercive power into a tool for incumbent protection.
