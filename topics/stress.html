Stress

The gap between what is happening and what you think should be happening.

Stress is the difference between what you can control and what you try to control.

The mind creates stress by fighting reality. Acceptance is the first step to peace.

Stress is not the event. It is the interpretation of the event. Change the interpretation.

The body keeps the score of stress. Listen before it breaks.

Pressure is external. Stress is internal. You cannot control the pressure, but you can change the stress.

The present moment is the only place without stress. The past is guilt. The future is anxiety.

Stress is a signal that you are alive and engaged. The question is whether the signal is too loud.

Breathe. The breath is the bridge between the autonomic and the voluntary. Use it.

The antidote to stress is not less work but more meaning. Meaninglessness is the true stressor.

Stress is the friction between expectations and reality. Reduce expectations or change reality.

The person who stresses about stress suffers twice. Accept stress, then act.

Resilience is not the absence of stress but the ability to return to baseline after disruption.

Most stress comes from trying to control the uncontrollable. Let go of the outcome.

The body is a stress barometer. The mind is a stress amplifier. The heart is the reset button.

Stress is not a sign of weakness. It is a sign that you are pushing against your edge.

The cure for stress is not escape but engagement. Dive into the work, not away from it.

Stress is the tax you pay for caring about something. Make sure the care is worth the cost.

The mind that is always preparing for disaster never enjoys the present calm.

Stress is a habit of thought. Like any habit, it can be changed with practice.

The greatest stressor is the belief that you should not be stressed. Accept the stress.

Stress is the alarm system. Do not disable it. Calibrate it.

The antidote to stress is not relaxation. It is trust. Trust in your ability to handle the outcome, whatever it is.
