Hindsight Bias

The illusion that past events were predictable all along

Hindsight bias is the tendency to see past events as having been predictable, even when they weren't.

Life is understood backwards, but must be lived forwards. Hindsight bias makes us forget the difference.

Hindsight bias is why everyone becomes a genius after the fact.

After an event occurs, we overestimate our ability to have foreseen it. This is hindsight bias.

Hindsight bias makes history seem inevitable when it was actually contingent on countless small decisions.

We rewrite the past to make it consistent with the present, erasing the uncertainty that actually existed.

Hindsight bias is why "I knew it all along" feels true even when it's demonstrably false.

The future is unpredictable, but the past is perfectly predictable - once it's already happened.

Hindsight bias makes us overly critical of past decisions made under uncertainty.

We judge yesterday's decisions with today's information, forgetting that today's information didn't exist yesterday.

Hindsight bias is the cognitive trick that turns chance into destiny.

Historians suffer from hindsight bias: they see patterns and inevitability where there was only chaos and choice.

Hindsight bias makes us think we're better predictors than we actually are.

The best way to combat hindsight bias is to keep a decision journal, recording what you knew and thought at the time.

Hindsight bias is why we blame victims for not seeing what "clearly" was coming.

In hindsight, everything looks obvious. In foresight, nothing is.

Hindsight bias erases the fog of uncertainty that surrounds every real-time decision.

We are all Monday morning quarterbacks in the game of life, thanks to hindsight bias.

Hindsight bias makes us overconfident about our ability to predict future events.

The past seems simple because we've collapsed all the branching possibilities into a single known outcome.

Hindsight bias is the reason we think we could have avoided the mistakes we see others make.

Hindsight bias turns lucky guesses into brilliant insights and reasonable risks into foolish gambles.

The future is a fog; the past is a clear sky. Hindsight bias makes us forget the fog.

To overcome hindsight bias, ask yourself: "What did I actually know at the time, and what could I have reasonably predicted?"

Hindsight bias is the silent editor of our memories, making the past more coherent than it ever was.
