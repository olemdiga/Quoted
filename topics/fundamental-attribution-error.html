Fundamental Attribution Error

Blaming character for what circumstance caused

The fundamental attribution error is our tendency to attribute others' behavior to their character while attributing our own to circumstances.

When you cut someone off in traffic, it's because you're late. When someone cuts you off, they're a terrible person.

We judge ourselves by our intentions and others by their actions.

The fundamental attribution error is the reason we believe the poor are lazy and the rich are hardworking, ignoring the circumstances that shaped both.

If you want to understand someone's behavior, look at their situation, not just their personality.

The fundamental attribution error makes us overestimate disposition and underestimate situation.

We are all products of our environment, but we only see that truth in ourselves, not in others.

The fundamental attribution error is why we have more compassion for ourselves than for strangers.

When explaining behavior, ask: "What would I do in their exact situation?" rather than "What kind of person does that?"

The fundamental attribution error is the cognitive foundation of prejudice.

We forgive ourselves easily because we know our own story. We judge others harshly because we don't know theirs.

The fundamental attribution error disappears when we learn someone's full context - which we rarely do.

To overcome the fundamental attribution error, assume that everyone is fighting a battle you know nothing about.

The fundamental attribution error explains why we think our successes are earned and our failures are bad luck - while reversing this for others.

We are all situational beings, but we only grant ourselves that complexity.

The fundamental attribution error is why "walk a mile in their shoes" is such powerful advice.

Character is revealed over time, not in single actions. The fundamental attribution error ignores this truth.

The fundamental attribution error is the cognitive shortcut that saves us the effort of understanding context.

When you find yourself judging someone harshly, ask: "What context am I missing?"

The fundamental attribution error is why first impressions are so sticky - we lock in a character judgment and ignore later context.

To be fair to others, grant them the same situational excuses you grant yourself.

The fundamental attribution error is the reason we think we're more complex than everyone else.

Every villain is the hero of their own story, shaped by circumstances we cannot see.

The fundamental attribution error is the enemy of empathy and the ally of division.

Understanding the fundamental attribution error is the first step toward genuine compassion.
