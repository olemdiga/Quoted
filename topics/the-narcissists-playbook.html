The Narcissist's Playbook

The predictable patterns of manipulation and control

The narcissist's playbook is simple: idealize, devalue, discard - and when you try to leave, repeat.

Love bombing is not love; it's the opening move in a game designed to make you dependent.

The narcissist doesn't see people; they see mirrors that either reflect their greatness or need to be shattered.

Gaslighting is the narcissist's favorite tool: making you doubt your own reality so they can define it for you.

Triangulation brings a third party into the dynamic, ensuring you never feel secure enough to challenge them.

The narcissist's apology is never an apology; it's a reset button dressed in words that sound like remorse.

Projection is the narcissist's confession: they accuse you of exactly what they're doing.

The silent treatment is not withdrawal; it's punishment for failing to provide adequate supply.

Narcissists don't have relationships; they have audiences. And the show must always be about them.

Hoovering is the narcissist's way of proving they still have power over you, long after you've tried to leave.

The narcissist's playbook works because they study what you need and offer a counterfeit version until you're hooked.

Boundaries are the narcissist's enemy; they represent limits to their control and access to your supply.

The narcissist doesn't want you to heal; they want you functional enough to serve but broken enough to stay.

Intermittent reinforcement is the narcissist's secret weapon: unpredictable rewards create addiction.

The narcissist's playbook relies on your hope that the good version of them is the real one. It's not.

Narcissists isolate you from support systems because they know isolated prey is easier to control.

Playing the victim is the narcissist's trump card: when cornered, they become the wounded one.

The narcissist's rage is not about what you did; it's about your failure to reflect back their desired image.

Future faking is the narcissist's way of keeping you invested in a tomorrow that never comes.

The narcissist's playbook is predictable once you know the patterns - and knowing is the first step to freedom.

Narcissists don't change because they don't believe they need to; the problem is always you.

Stonewalling is the narcissist's fortress: a wall of silence designed to make you feel invisible.

The narcissist's playbook is a manual for extracting supply while giving nothing of substance in return.

Recovery from narcissistic abuse requires recognizing that the love you thought you had was a performance.

The only way to win the narcissist's game is to stop playing.
