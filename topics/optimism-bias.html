Optimism Bias

The tendency to overestimate the likelihood of positive outcomes

Optimism bias is why we think we'll beat the odds, even when the odds are clear.

We underestimate how long projects will take, how much they'll cost, and how hard they'll be. That's optimism bias.

Optimism bias is the reason we start businesses knowing that most fail, believing we'll be the exception.

Optimism bias keeps us trying when realism would have us quit. It's a feature, not a bug.

Optimism bias is why marriage rates remain high despite divorce statistics. Everyone thinks it won't happen to them.

Optimism bias is the engine of progress, but it's also the architect of disappointment.

We are all above average in our own minds, especially when it comes to our future prospects.

Optimism bias explains why we don't save enough for retirement. The future self feels like a different person who will magically figure it out.

Optimism bias is the cognitive distortion that makes the planning fallacy possible.

Optimism bias is why we think we can change our habits next week, even though we've failed every week before.

Optimism bias is the reason we believe we're better drivers than average, better investors than average, and better judges of character than average.

Optimism bias is the fuel of entrepreneurship. Without it, no one would take the leap.

Optimism bias makes us overconfident about our ability to predict and control the future.

Optimism bias is the reason we think bad things happen to other people, not to us.

Optimism bias is the rose-colored glasses through which we view our own future.

Optimism bias is adaptive: it keeps us hopeful and motivated in the face of uncertainty.

Optimism bias is why we're shocked when things go wrong, even when the warning signs were clear.

Optimism bias is the belief that tomorrow will be better than today, even when there's no evidence.

Optimism bias is why we think we can finish the project by Friday when every similar project took two weeks.

Optimism bias is the cognitive tax we pay for hope. And hope is worth the price.

Optimism bias is the reason "this time will be different" feels true, even when history says otherwise.

Optimism bias is why we believe we can beat the market, beat the casino, beat the odds.

Optimism bias is the gap between what we expect and what actually happens.

Optimism bias is not a flaw to eliminate but a bias to calibrate.

Optimism bias is the whisper that says "you can do it" when logic says "probably not." Sometimes it's right.
