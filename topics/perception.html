Perception

The window through which we view reality - and the fingerprints we leave on the glass

We don't see things as they are, we see them as we are.

The eye sees only what the mind is prepared to comprehend.

Reality is merely an illusion, albeit a very persistent one.

Perception is not reality, but, honestly, it's what matters. It's everything.

The map is not the territory, but we navigate by these imperfect maps we've drawn in our minds.

What we perceive is not nature itself, but nature exposed to our method of questioning.

The mind is its own place, and in itself can make a heaven of hell, a hell of heaven.

We are more often frightened than hurt; and we suffer more from imagination than from reality.

Perception is a creative act - we don't just receive the world, we actively construct it.

The greatest deception men suffer is from their own opinions.

We don't perceive the world - we perceive our nervous system's model of the world.

Change the way you look at things and the things you look at change.

Our senses don't deceive us; our judgments do.

Perception is not passive reception - it's active interrogation of the world.

The world is full of magic things, patiently waiting for our senses to grow sharper.

We see what we expect to see, and we're often blind to what we don't anticipate.

Perception is the editor of reality - it cuts out what it deems unimportant.

The doors of perception are not fixed - they swing wider with attention and narrower with distraction.

We don't experience reality directly - we experience our brain's best guess about reality.

What you see and hear depends a good deal on where you are standing; it also depends on what sort of person you are.

Perception is the first act of creation - before we can change the world, we must first perceive it differently.

The mind is a filter, not a mirror - it shows us not what's there, but what we're prepared to see.

We are prisoners of our perceptions until we learn to question them.

Perception is the interface between consciousness and the cosmos.

The world appears different when you stop telling yourself stories about what you're seeing.

Our perceptions are hypotheses about the world, constantly being tested and updated.

To see is to forget the name of the thing one sees.

Perception creates our reality long before reality confirms our perceptions.

The most difficult subjects can be explained to the most slow-witted man if he has not formed any idea of them already; but the simplest thing cannot be made clear to the most intelligent man if he is firmly persuaded that he knows already.

We don't see the world - we see the story we tell ourselves about the world.
