Triangulation

The geometry of manipulation

Triangulation is bringing a third party into a two-person dynamic to avoid direct communication and maintain control.

When someone won't talk to you directly but will talk about you to everyone else, you're being triangulated.

Triangulation turns relationships into chess games where you're always playing against an opponent you can't see.

The manipulator uses triangulation to recruit allies, isolate targets, and avoid accountability - all at once.

"Everyone agrees with me" is the triangulator's favorite phrase - and the "everyone" is never in the room.

Triangulation is the art of making you compete for someone who has already decided the outcome.

In dysfunctional families, triangulation is the mechanism that prevents two people from ever resolving their issues directly.

The triangulator's power comes from controlling information flow between the other two points of the triangle.

Triangulation creates jealousy, insecurity, and competition where direct communication would create clarity.

The antidote to triangulation is direct communication: "I'll talk to them myself" breaks the triangle instantly.

Triangulation is why gossip feels intimate but is actually isolating - you're being recruited, not connected.

The triangulator plays both sides, ensuring that neither of the other two ever compares notes.

In the workplace, triangulation looks like a manager telling you what "others" said about you without naming names.

Triangulation is the narcissist's way of ensuring you never feel secure enough to challenge them.

The triangle is stable because each point is kept slightly off-balance, always seeking the triangulator's approval.

Triangulation creates drama where none exists, then positions the triangulator as the peacemaker.

Refuse to carry messages between two people. That's not helping; that's enabling triangulation.

The triangulator's favorite tool is the hypothetical consensus: "People are saying..." without ever naming the people.

Triangulation is the opposite of transparency. It thrives in shadows and dies in direct light.

Recognizing triangulation is the first step to refusing to participate in it.

Triangulation makes you doubt your own perceptions by constantly introducing alternative narratives from unseen sources.

Healthy relationships are dyads that can tolerate a third without becoming triangles.

Triangulation is the architecture of drama - and some people can't live without being the architect.

The way out of triangulation is to make the implicit explicit and the indirect direct.

Triangulation works because we're more comfortable talking about people than talking to them.
